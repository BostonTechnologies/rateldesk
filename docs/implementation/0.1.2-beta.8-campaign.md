# 0.1.2-beta.8 owner HTTP-MCP campaign

This is the technical acceptance contract for a fresh **0.1.2-beta.8** campaign on
the operator's deployment. A prepared template, a successful build, passing unit
tests or a previous beta campaign does not establish a deployed production pass.
The final result must identify the authentication mode that was actually tested.

The executable requirements are in
[`beta8-campaign-contract.json`](../../tools/ci/beta8-campaign-contract.json).
The offline [receipt validator](../../tools/ci/validate-beta8-campaign.py) checks
release identity, every released operation, 103 domain cases, all 86 substantive
historical obligations, a bounded soak and cleanup. Its
[unit tests](../../tools/ci/test-beta8-campaign.py) use synthetic data to verify
that incomplete and false-positive receipts are rejected; those fixtures are
never campaign evidence.

## Scope and authority

Use the exact released [operation catalog](../contracts/mcp-operation-catalog.json)
and live `tools/list` response to enumerate operations. The denominator is the
complete beta.8 export, including every new operation. The frozen
[beta.7 catalog](../contracts/mcp-operation-catalog-beta7.json) retains the older
155-operation denominator; it must not replace the current scope.

The previous campaign's 53 blocked operation rows and the historical register's
86 obligations describe different things. Operation coverage measures callable
capabilities; the obligations include regression families, isolation, mail,
provider callbacks, history and performance. Each denominator remains explicit.
The new campaign maps every substantive case in the
[original register](0.1.2-beta.6-acceptance/closure-register.json). It does not
invent acknowledgements or signatures for named people. The original register,
validator and frozen test fixtures remain unchanged as historical evidence.

Authorize a disposable, account-bound HTTP-MCP credential with the explicit
`owner-administration` grant through the existing browser pairing flow. Follow
[owner administration configuration](../mcp-http/configuration.md#approve-an-agent-to-administer-the-instance)
and [access policy](../security/rbac.md). Verify `helpdesk_auth.access` at execution
time: `isAuthenticated: true`, `isHelpdeskAdmin: true`, and
`grantKind: owner-administration`. A credential with `*` permissions or an
administrator account behind an ordinary credential does not establish this
authority. Also retain an ordinary scoped credential whose observed access has
`isHelpdeskAdmin: false` and `grantKind: ordinary`.

Owner authority enables the permitted administrative actions on behalf of the
account owner. Continue to enforce purpose, exact MCP resource, approved scope,
account state, expiry, revocation and current owner authority. Use distinct
customer, support, approver and foreign-tenant actors where required. An owner
must not impersonate a designated approver or convert an ordinary credential into
an owner credential. Revocation and role removal tests use separate disposable
credentials and accounts so the campaign can continue with its primary owner.

The adapters use named, typed operations over canonical routes. New families
cover automation forms/tasks/approvals, system connections, attachments, mail and
email settings, support administration, SLA, datasets and tenant/account
administration, automation rules, assets, instance/tenant branding and background
worker settings. The original ticket, incident, request, change, notification,
AI report/log, customer and administrative operations are also mandatory. There
is no arbitrary URL or raw write proxy.

## Establish the release and prerequisites

Before mutations, record the exact `v0.1.2-beta.8` source revision, downloaded
release manifest, archive checksums and immutable container digests. Verify the
running API, Web and HTTP-MCP components each report the candidate version and
source revision and use the manifest digest. Verify the released CLI and stdio
archive for the laptop's actual platform, including the archive checksum and
fresh-process version/source output. The release distribution check covers all
published architectures; a laptop campaign must not claim it executed them all.

Capture the live `tools/list` schemas, `helpdesk_capabilities.get` catalog hash
and the exact released catalog export. The validator recomputes native semantic
catalog and schema hashes, compares live schemas to the export, retains all
beta.7 operation IDs and requires the beta.8 catalog revision. Another beta,
a locally rebuilt substitute or a deployment with drift cannot pass.

Prepare synthetic campaign-owned records and record initial state before edits.
Demonstrate these prerequisites with bounded read-only checks:

- A currently authorized owner and ordinary scoped caller, two tenant scopes,
  linked customer identities, support members, and distinct designated approvers.
- The explicitly configured paired automation system, organization/tenant mapping,
  actual job/request targets and a real AI provider, including a configured native
  conversation provider on PostgreSQL for the exported native-chat operations. Record provider execution,
  invocation, binding and callback identities for later exact correlation.
- An isolated SMTP capture service and controlled transport failure fixtures,
  saved sender/mailbox settings, and known initial worker state. No fixture mail
  may leave the capture environment. Inbound forwarding uses the actual supported
  ingestion route; public ticket worklogs are the supported reply action.
- Every enabled provider operation in the released catalog, including an actual
  Graph dataset synchronization fixture when that operation is exported. An
  unconfigured integration is a missing prerequisite, not a successful operation.
- Read access for independent API, CLI, UI and storage verification, and immutable
  candidate CI test reports for the specifically designated implementation cases.
- A timestamped p95 latency budget declared before the campaign starts. Preserve
  correct summary counts while measuring latency; never choose the budget after
  seeing results.

Record a missing prerequisite and its affected case IDs. Continue independent
work, fix authorized configuration and rerun affected cases when possible. A
remaining product blocker or unexplained failure prevents `PASS`. Do not switch
API targets, authentication modes or providers silently to manufacture coverage.

required evidence surfaces. This table explains the resulting behavior to test.

it actually proves each mapped assertion; repeated calls do not create new cases.

### Operations and execution order

1. Complete preflight and identity observations. Run the eight historical repaired
   operations first: notification summary, the four ticket/task lists, and the
   incident/request/change timelines. Preserve their isolation checks and the
   complete original regression-family requirements.
2. For every applicable operation, capture a fresh HTTP-MCP request and structured
   outcome. For each operation requiring confirmation, capture a separate
   unconfirmed preview with independent evidence of no effect, then execute with
   `confirm: true` and verify the expected postcondition. Administrative operations
   also require an actual authorization denial from an ordinary scoped caller.
3. Complete all domain cases and all 86 historical obligation mappings. A preview,
   status-only check, HTTP 200, denial, empty selection, `updated: 0` or unchanged
   record does not prove a successful mutation. Reads must have meaningful
   fixture-specific assertions; verify paging, counts, filters and identities.
4. Record `functionalCompletedAt`, then run a read-only soak for at least 900 and
   at most 3,600 seconds. Collect 30–10,000 samples with no unobserved interval over
   60 seconds, unique captured responses and no unexpected failures. Use a fresh
   JSON-RPC ID for each call. Record latency per request and calculate p95 against
   the previously declared budget. Keep mutations outside this soak.
5. Restore campaign changes and verify cleanup after the soak. Preserve terminal
   history and captured evidence. Revoke temporary credentials, stop temporary
   jobs, restore sender/mailbox/worker and branding/runtime settings and record every fixture's retained
   or deleted disposition. Verify unrelated records are unchanged and reconcile
   all uncertain effects before the final result.

### Mail, concurrency and recovery details

A queued worklog or mail send is not a delivery receipt. Compare every expected
recipient and delivery attempt with SMTP capture plus independent stored history.
Show definite rejection, partial recipient acceptance and an uncertain transport
outcome as separate states. Never resend an uncertain effect automatically.
Selected retry/cancel/remove actions require exact fixture IDs and a reason.
Current-revision, proven-undelivered and changed-route retries require the actual
previewed sender/mailbox revisions and fence; retain independently collected
nondelivery evidence where that route requires it. A stale fence must reject
without affecting the queue. Historical imports select at most 25 distinct
messages from the preview, and must not duplicate an existing receipt.

For automation retries, prove a new provider execution ID for every retried
attempt and preserve previous terminal attempts. Following a timeout or 409,
read the actual task, invocation, queue or dataset before deciding whether a
new action is appropriate. Reconcile success that occurred despite a lost
response; do not repeat a side effect solely because its response was uncertain.

For change and request approvals, use actual different designated actors. Change
approvals have the canonical Pending/Reviewed states: complete each designated
review and separately exercise the authorized manager's return-to-Draft lifecycle.
Request approvals have explicit approve/reject branches; exercise both on separate
disposable workflows. Submit a same-decision
retry and concurrent decisions/lifecycle changes, then independently read the winning
stored decision, terminal workflow state and notification outbox. Exactly one
accepted transition may produce its side effects. Authorization to administer
records does not authorize impersonating the designated approver.

For ticket lookup and assignment, verify the enabled organization/customer
choices for each module and exact support-coverage eligibility. Exercise repeated
filter selectors with bounded values and compare UI labels. Retain an existing
assignment after removing its eligibility, then verify current-assignee readback
still resolves the stored user while new assignment selection excludes them.
Deny access to an unrelated ticket or organization.

For incident relationships, create both related and duplicate links on disposable
incidents in one organization. Read both incidents, the persisted link and copied
listeners; duplicate links resolve the source and complete its SLA. Delete the
selected link and verify that removal does not reopen the source or remove copied
listeners. A duplicate creation or uncertain response requires readback before
another action. Exercise silent bulk deletion and marketing/spam resolution on
separate selected fixtures; verify every selected and untouched incident, completed
SLA state where applicable, and the absence of requester mail in the capture.
Retain negative tenant/selection checks and do not infer success from counts alone.

For native AI chat, first inspect capabilities, explicitly confirm opening a
conversation, then exercise message submission, offered interaction responses,
history pagination and creation of a new idle conversation. Use real provider
receipts to demonstrate uncertain-turn reconcile, stop-waiting and acknowledged
abandon without duplicate sending; preserve conversation, message, resolution and
interaction IDs. A deployment that does not support native chat cannot claim
those operations passed; record the capability and remaining prerequisite.

For business administration, create/read/edit/delete a real asset and automation
rule through MCP and compare storage plus UI. Preserve route identifiers and
explicit organization assignment, including an intentionally unassigned asset.
Read instance branding before full replacement; demonstrate explicit reset and
preservation of deployment-owned values. Upload a bounded tenant logo using the
canonical numeric branding tenant identifier, then verify rendered/readback state.
Read background-worker runtime before and after an explicit enablement change,
verify recurring job reconciliation, and restore the starting configuration.

For datasets, exercise concurrent writes through the authenticated operator
route and the established ingestion route. Keys are case-insensitively unique;
`A` and `a` must not poison later reads or silently lose a concurrent update.
A serialization conflict returns 409 for explicit readback and reconciliation.
Verify rejected/oversized/duplicate batches change nothing. Existing legacy rows
with conflicting stored keys require a reviewed full replacement with
`deleteMissing: true`; first enumerate and retain the intended complete dataset,
then confirm that replacement and independently verify each retained/deleted row.
Ordinary partial updates must not silently repair or delete legacy conflicts.

Confidential fields may be entered only in the explicit authorized write operation
and must be absent from ordinary results, errors, previews and audit output.
Retain secret references and nonsecret verification facts in evidence. New pairing,
activation and API-key values may be returned once by their dedicated issuance
operation; consume them securely and redact them before storing campaign captures.
Preserve actual nonsecret request/response fields and record the redaction policy.
Do not include account passwords or usable tokens in receipts or attachments.

## Fixed capability exclusions and authentication scope

Every current operation receives exactly one row. Only the following operations
may be marked `EXCLUDED`; they are never included in `operationsPassed`. Each
requires a fresh negative guard call plus an independent assertion named
`explicit_capability_guard`. The Local-only entries also require positive evidence
of an exact supported Local equivalent and an independently observed Local mode.

| Operation | Applicable exclusion | Required positive equivalent in Local mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `helpdesk_config.set` | HTTP host configuration persistence is not exposed to a remote caller. | None; capture the HTTP guard. |
| `helpdesk_config.unset` | HTTP host configuration persistence is not exposed to a remote caller. | None; capture the HTTP guard. |
| `helpdesk_admin_mutations.provision_user` | The self-provisioning action targets an external identity provider. | `helpdesk_tenant_admin.create_local_account` |
| `helpdesk_admin_mutations.customer_invite` | External customer identity action on a Local deployment. | `helpdesk_tenant_admin.create_local_account` or `helpdesk_tenant_admin.reset_customer_login` |
| `helpdesk_admin_mutations.customer_resend_invite` | External customer identity action on a Local deployment. | `helpdesk_tenant_admin.reset_local_account` or `helpdesk_tenant_admin.reset_customer_login` |
| `helpdesk_admin_mutations.customer_disable_login` | External customer identity action on a Local deployment. | `helpdesk_tenant_admin.disable_local_account` |
| `helpdesk_admin_mutations.customer_sync_authentik` | External customer identity action on a Local deployment. | `helpdesk_customers.auth_status` or `helpdesk_tenant_admin.reset_customer_login` |

An Authentik campaign must positively execute those five external operations
against a real configured provider. A Local campaign must positively execute the
Local alternatives; no foreign identity provider is required merely to administer
a Local business account. For Local runs, exactly the historical
`B6-N01/external_shared_principal_binding` and
`B6-N01/external_admin_without_ai_scheme` assertions may use passing tests tied to
the candidate source as `implementation` cases. Keep this classification visible.
It does not claim that an external authentication session was exercised locally.
The historical SQLite/PostgreSQL mail regression cases also retain their original
implementation classification and require real passing candidate test reports.

A successful validator result says **Local owner HTTP-MCP capability campaign** or
**Authentik owner HTTP-MCP capability campaign**, as observed, and reports
`allAuthenticationModesValidated: false`. Acceptance across all supported modes
requires separately recorded, successful mode-specific campaigns. Do not label
an unavailable provider, incomplete administrative action or missing mail/approval
fixture as an additional capability exclusion.

## Create and validate the private receipt

Use the catalog from the exact release under test. The template command refuses
to overwrite an existing receipt and sets all operations to `NOT_RUN`.

```bash
python3 tools/ci/validate-beta8-campaign.py template \
  --catalog docs/contracts/mcp-operation-catalog.json \
  --output /tmp/rateldesk-beta8-campaign/campaign.json
```

Complete the receipt with fresh captures, then validate it against an independently
downloaded release manifest and the exact released catalog:

```bash
python3 tools/ci/validate-beta8-campaign.py validate \
  --catalog docs/contracts/mcp-operation-catalog.json \
  --manifest /tmp/rateldesk-beta8-campaign/release-manifest.json \
  --receipt /tmp/rateldesk-beta8-campaign/campaign.json \
  --evidence-root /tmp/rateldesk-beta8-campaign
```

Use a private durable evidence directory for the real campaign; `/tmp` above is
only an example. All referenced files must be beneath the evidence root. Each
reference has exactly `path` and lowercase `sha256`; paths are relative, have no
parent traversal and contain no symlinks. The validator hashes each file, limits
JSON to 8 MiB, individual evidence to 64 MiB, the bundle to 1 GiB and references
to 20,000 files. Large captures should be bounded to the precise synthetic fixture.
It makes no network requests and does not mutate a deployment.

### Receipt objects

All timestamps are timezone-aware ISO 8601 values. `campaign` supplies a unique
ID, exact version/tag/source, observed authentication mode, start time, functional
completion and final completion. Capture observations and functional cases inside
those intervals. `productBlockers` must be empty and `unresolvedFailures` zero to
pass. Keep a failing or unfinished receipt as `NOT_READY` or `NOT_RUN`.

| Object | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| `releaseManifest` | A hashed copy of the same immutable manifest supplied through `--manifest`. |
| `deployment` | `observedAt`, `authenticationMode`, an `authenticationModeEvidence` JSON capture, `components` keyed exactly `api`, `web`, `mcp-http`, and `nativeTools` keyed `cli`, `mcp-stdio`. Components contain version/source/digest plus a hashed observation of those actual fields. Native tools contain asset name, archive SHA256, version/source and a hashed observation with `archiveSha256`. |
| `catalog` | Hashed `export`, `wireDiscovery` and `capabilities` captures plus exact `catalogHash` and `schemaHash`. `wireDiscovery` is the real `tools/list` response; capabilities is the structured capability result. |
| `actors` | Stable fixture ID, kind (`owner`, `scoped`, or another explicit fixture kind), `observedAt` and hashed `accessEvidence`. Use actual `helpdesk_auth.access` results for owner/scoped observations. |
| `cases` | A mapping from case ID to the hashed JSON case receipt described below. |
| `operations` | Exactly one row per exported operation ID. Applicable rows have `state: PASS`, `positiveCaseId`, and separate `previewCaseId`/`denialCaseId` where required. Fixed exclusions have `state: EXCLUDED`, `guardCaseId` and the required Local `equivalentCaseId`. |
| `scenarios` | Exactly the ten contract IDs, each mapping every required case name to its actual case receipt ID. |
| `historicalObligations` | Exactly the 86 contract IDs, each mapping every original substantive case name. The assertion ID is `historical:<obligationId>/<caseName>`. |
| `soak` | Context, start/completion, hashed sample array, total request count, zero unexpected failures and a hashed predeclared budget containing `declaredAt`, `metric: p95`, `limitMs`. Each sample has time `at`, read-only operation ID, latency, `unexpectedFailure: false` and its distinct hashed response. |
| `cleanup` | Context, observation time after the soak, `independent: true` and independently verifiable assertions for all nine cleanup requirements in the contract. |

Every case, soak and cleanup receipt carries the identical `context` object:
`campaignId`, `version`, `tag`, `sourceRevision`, `deploymentSha256`,
`catalogFileSha256`, `catalogHash` and `schemaHash`. The two file hashes identify
the actual deployment receipt and released export bytes, not their filenames.

A deployed case contains `id`, context, `outcome: PASS`, `kind` (`positive`,
`expected_negative` or `preview`), start/completion, `origin: fresh_deployed`,
`transport: http`, exact operation ID, actor ID, hashed request/response,
`effectObserved` and a `postcondition`. Preserve the JSON-RPC request method
`tools/call`, tool name, operation argument and actual response ID. JSON-RPC error
captures and recorded HTTP 401/403 responses can support negative cases. A
malformed input denial cannot substitute for an authorization boundary denial.

Postconditions require `independent: true`, a fresh `observedAt`, and one or more
assertions. Each assertion supplies `id`, `surface`, a hashed JSON `evidence`
reference, a JSON `pointer` and the exact typed `expected` value. For example,
`pointer: /delivery/status`, `expected: Cancelled` must match that observed field
in the captured independent readback. The validator evaluates the pointer; an
assertion string alone is insufficient. The evidence must differ from the tool
request and response captures. Surfaces are `api`, `cli`, `ui`, `storage`,
`provider`, `transport_capture`, `mcp` and `test_report`. Preserve the expected
provider/SMTP/storage surfaces for domain cases. Binary screenshots and reports
can be attached as `supportingEvidence` with their surface and hashed reference.

An `implementation` case instead has `origin: candidate_ci`, the same candidate
source context, a hashed `testResults` TRX and explicit `assertionIds`. Every
result in the referenced TRX must be passed, with at least one test and no skipped
results. Include the real build/test provenance linking that report to the
candidate source; record its original CI time separately. The mapping time is the
fresh campaign interval. This path is allowed only where the contract explicitly
requires implementation evidence or the two Local-mode historical exceptions.

The validator verifies artifact integrity, complete mappings, recorded values
and identity consistency. It cannot determine whether an operator fabricated a
capture or whether an assertion was chosen to avoid an important behavior. Keep
raw nonsecret captures and fixture provenance reviewable, use real independent
reads, and inspect the substantive results before making a production claim.

## Handoff and result

Deliver the immutable release identifiers and checksums, full operation result
matrix, all domain and historical case mappings, validator output, soak metrics,
cleanup disposition and sanitized issue-to-evidence summary. Report positives,
fixed exclusions, failures and blockers separately. Retain historical evidence
as historical; do not reuse old responses as a fresh beta.8 pass.

Keep raw deployment captures, tenant/user identifiers, hostnames and credentials
out of the public repository, issues and release assets. Publish only generic
code, contracts, sanitized findings and artifact hashes. A remaining failure
must name its affected operation/case, exact candidate identity and observed
result, with a private evidence reference and the work required to rerun it.
